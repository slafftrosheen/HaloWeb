Home / Your AI

You choose where Halo thinks.

Every answer is worked out by an AI model somewhere. Halo lets you decide where: on your phone, on your own computer, or with a provider you pick. You can mix them, and change your mind.

Three choices

Choose a place to compare

On this phone

Good for
Privacy, quick everyday tasks, working without a connection once a model is downloaded.
Trade-offs
Phone models are small. Downloads take storage (hundreds of MB to a few GB), and heavy use costs battery.
Worth knowing
Halo checks how well a model fits your phone. Downloading a model doesn’t switch it on — you test it, then choose it.

On your own machine

Good for
Bigger models than a phone can run, while your requests stay on your own network.
Trade-offs
You need a computer that’s switched on and set up. Today that means running a compatible AI server yourself.
Worth knowing
HaloLinux, a Halo home node for a Raspberry Pi 5 or a Linux PC, is in development. There’s no release yet. More about HaloLinux

A provider you choose

Good for
The most capable models, image understanding and long documents.
Trade-offs
What you send goes to that provider under its privacy policy, and it may charge you. That cost is between you and the provider — it isn’t a Halo fee.
Worth knowing
Halo never picks the cloud for you. Some provider setups still have known issues, so each one is tested before we list it as working.
Different jobs, different models

Halo uses the right model for each job.

You don’t have to know model names. Halo sorts the work into a few jobs, and you can let it choose or pick for each one.

JobWhat it means for you
Answers & chatWriting replies, explaining, summarising.
Understanding requestsWorking out which action you mean, and asking when it isn’t sure.
SeeingReading a photo, a document or what’s on screen.
HearingTurning your speech into text.
SpeakingReading answers aloud.
FindingSearching your notes and memory by meaning.
Advanced notes: models being tested on Android
  • Understanding requests: Qwen3 0.6B (LiteRT, INT4) is the first suggestion for small on-device tool and intent work. It’s only given this job after it passes Halo’s checks on your phone.
  • Answers: LFM2.5 is an optional on-device chat model, English first.
  • Seeing: multimodal models are supported where the phone and model pass qualification.
  • Finding: on-device text embeddings for search.
  • No model listing is a performance promise. Results vary by phone, memory and model.

Honest limits

What depends on your phone.

Memory

Larger models need more RAM. Halo warns you before you download one that’s too big for your phone.

Speed

On-device answers can be slower than the cloud, especially on older phones. We don’t promise response times.

Abilities

Not every model can use tools, see images or speak every language. Halo shows what a model has passed on your phone.
