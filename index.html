One intelligence across your phone and everything around it.

Halo is a personal cognitive runtime. It connects your phone, your AI models, your workspace and the devices around you — glasses, earbuds, watches, rings, cameras, robots — into one coherent assistant. Local-first, open source, no account required. Halo is a useful phone assistant on its own; wearables extend its reach.

How it works

One loop: capture, understand, remember, act

Halo is not a chatbot with a history — it is a cognitive workspace. Everything it hears or captures can flow through the same loop.

1

Capture

Speak, type, or use a connected device — glasses, earbuds, whatever is at hand. Photos, voice notes and conversations land in one place.

2

Understand

Speech-to-text, vision and reasoning run on your phone where possible — through the AI provider you choose.

3

Work

Ask questions, draft notes, set reminders, control your phone. Halo explains what it did and why.

4

Remember

What matters can be promoted to memory — explicitly, with provenance. Nothing is remembered behind your back.

5

Revisit

Notes, conversations and captures stay in your Notebook — the persistent cognitive workspace. Searchable and exportable; your data stays yours.

6

Act

Set a timer, call a contact, capture on the go. Every action shows its result — no silent side effects.

Privacy & ownership

Your assistant should outlive its vendor

Halo is built local-first: on-device processing is the default direction. When a task does need a bigger model, you decide which provider gets it — and nothing in Halo requires an account or a Halo-operated cloud.

Perpetual ownership

A legitimately installed Halo keeps working with just your host OS and a local model — even if every Halo-operated online service disappeared tomorrow.

No account. No helper apps.

No mandatory login or cloud. And no required Tasker, automation apps or vendor companions — native OS APIs first. External integrations extend Halo; they never complete it.

Your models, your data

Local llama.cpp models by default; Ollama or OpenRouter as explicit, optional choices. Notes, memories and models belong to you — inspectable, exportable, deletable.

Devices

A capability-driven device ecosystem

Halo evaluates every device by what it can do — audio input, buttons, cameras, speakers — not by brand. Wearables are a major target, not the boundary: device support grows with verified evidence, so capabilities are documented when they are tested, not before.

Smart glasses & AR glasses

Capture photos and voice, hear answers back — your look-out into the world. HeyCyan is the current reference implementation.

Interaction-origin continuity

Answers return to where they began

If you speak through a wearable, Halo prefers that wearable for the response when its capabilities allow it — and falls back deliberately and visibly, never accidentally.

Glasses mic → glasses speakers

Capture and reply stay on the device where the conversation started, keeping context with the interaction.

Earbuds → earbuds

The same rule applies to any capable audio device — input and output stay together.

Phone → phone

No wearable? The phone handles everything — Halo remains a complete assistant without any wearable attached.

Halo Platform

A tracked base that carries Halo around

The Halo Platform is a motorised base with tank-style tracks and a pan servo. Your phone docks on top — running the full Halo runtime — and drives the platform, so your assistant can physically come to you, face you, and see the room.

Phone docks, Halo drives

Your phone snaps onto the platform and connects over Bluetooth/Wi-Fi. The docked app runs Halo as usual — plus a drive surface for the base.

Tracks + pan servo

Tank-style tracks handle floors and thresholds; the pan servo turns Halo to face you while it talks, listens, or captures.

Safety comes standard

Borrowing the BuddyBot contract: motors stay disarmed until armed, motion is bounded and throttled, release means stop, and telemetry on screen stays fresh or the drive stops.

Roadmap

Where Halo is heading

Halo is in early, active development. The plan below follows the project's published roadmap.

  • In progress

    The Halo app

    Home, Activity, Notebook, Devices and Settings — the working Android host.

  • In progress

    Host-native Android assistant

    Halo as a system-level assistant built on supported Android APIs — not an add-on that depends on other helper apps.

  • In progress

    Local inference by default

    Native llama.cpp is the designated default provider, with GGUF download integrity checks and preload while you speak.

  • Next

    Generalized wearable capability layer

    Devices join by capability — audio, buttons, cameras — starting with the verified glasses track, then earbuds, watches and beyond.

  • Next

    Dependable assistant actions

    Reminders, calls, messages and app control with receipts — firing an intent is never reported as completion.

  • Next

    Notebook expansion & Study

    Full workspace objects, durable memory with provenance — and Study, a guided-learning workflow (Ask → Hint → Explain → Check) built on the Notebook.

  • Planned

    Real hardware acceptance

    Every claim re-proven on physical devices: speaker output, battery, reconnects, latency. Evidence outranks assumptions.

  • Planned

    Cross-host groundwork

    The runtime is host-agnostic: Linux and Windows hosts are future targets. Android is the first host, not the only one.
