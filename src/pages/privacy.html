Private by default. Clear when it isn’t.

Plain answers to the questions you’d ask a friend who built an assistant: where does my stuff go, who can see it, and what does it cost?

Three places your information can be.

  • {{I:on_device}}

    Stays on your phone

    • Notes, files, saved chats and memory
    • Answers from models running on your phone
    • Local voices and speech recognition you install
    • Settings, permissions and provider keys (kept in Android’s secure storage)
  • {{I:cloud}}

    Leaves only if you choose

    • Requests you send to an AI provider you set up — under that provider’s privacy policy
    • Requests to your own computer on your network
    • Notes you add as context to a cloud request
    • Feedback you decide to email us
  • {{I:no_account}}

    Halo itself doesn’t collect

    • No Halo account or sign-up
    • No analytics, tracking or advertising
    • No Halo-run server that stores your data
    • No Halo-run AI service you’re required to use

Some things are naturally online: the source code lives on GitHub, this website is hosted, and some Android features come from Google’s own system services, under Google’s terms. Halo doesn’t add a data service of its own on top.

You stay in control

See and change what Halo can use.

Permissions, memory, storage and backups each have one clear place in Settings. Halo asks for Android permissions only when a task needs them.
