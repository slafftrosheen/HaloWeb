Outcomes

Six words Halo uses — and never stretches.

When an app doesn’t tell Halo whether something worked, Halo says so. It never shows a success it can’t check.

  • {{I:task}}ProposedHalo suggests an action. Nothing has happened.
  • {{I:approval}}ApprovedYou said yes. Halo can start.
  • {{I:act}}RunningWorking on it. Stop is always there.
  • {{I:check}}ConfirmedHalo checked the result and it worked.
  • {{I:failure}}FailedIt didn’t work. You get one clear next step.
  • {{I:unverified}}Not verifiedSent to the app, but Halo couldn’t check the result.
Staying in charge

Stop, retry, and the permissions you control.

{{I:stop}}

Stop means stop

Press Stop, or start talking, and Halo stops speaking and stops the task. What already happened is listed in Activity.

{{I:retry}}

One clear fix

When something fails, Halo offers one next step — try again, give a permission, or pick another way — not a wall of error text.

{{STATUS:available}}
{{I:permission}}

Your permissions

Halo asks for Android permissions only when a task needs them, and you can review or remove them in Settings.

{{STATUS:available}}

Halo in other apps

Halo can also be Android’s assistant: call it up over any app, ask about what’s on screen, and hand off to the full app when you need more. Each part of this is being tested on real phones before we show it as working.

{{STATUS:dev}}
Notifications

A private digest, only from apps you allow.

Halo can summarise notifications from the apps you choose. Nothing is read until you switch it on.
