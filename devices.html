Home / Devices

Your phone first. Other devices when you want them.

Halo isn’t tied to one gadget. It starts on the Android phone you already have. Everything else is optional — and the parts that aren’t ready yet are labelled that way.

Android phone

The first home for Halo.

Your phone runs the conversation, your notes and your settings. It can run AI models itself, or reach a computer or provider you choose. No glasses or other hardware needed.

Available in the alpha
Where this is heading

A private network of your own devices.

The idea: your phone, a small computer at home and, one day, other devices work together — sharing your notes and tasks directly with each other, with no Halo server in the middle. This is the direction, not something you can use today.

HaloLinux

An early home node for a Raspberry Pi or Linux PC.

HaloLinux is a separate, headless Halo host for ARM64 and x86-64 — a small computer that stays on at home. It’s early, and there’s no download yet.

In development

What’s in the source today

  • A command-line tool and a background service that starts, stops and recovers
  • Connects to an AI server you choose (OpenAI-compatible)
  • Conversations and memory stored locally, with full-text search
  • Audio checks, and records of agent runs that survive a crash

The latest HaloLinux source includes runtime and adapter reconciliation, durable agent-run storage and recovery. Hardware qualification and complete end-to-end verification are still pending; source changes alone are not a public Linux release.
